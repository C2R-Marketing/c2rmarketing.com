One-Page Lesson Plan: Ten Hebrew Words in One Sitting

For a home group, a Sunday-school class, or a family table. Total time: about 45 minutes. Materials: the trainer on a phone or laptop, and the printable pack (below) — one flashcard set per table.

Before you start (2 minutes)

Write "Hallelujah" on the board. Ask: "What does this word mean?" Take the answers. Most groups will say it means "praise God" — close, but it is a sentence, and one of its words is a name. Tell them: "By the end of tonight you'll know the name inside it — and nine more words like it."

The hook (8 minutes)

Start with Psalm 23: "The LORD is my shepherd." Show the Hebrew pair — Yehōvah Ro'i — and ask what changed when the sentence became two names instead of a title and a job description. Then Yah: finish the hook by opening the word they came in humming about.

The circuit (20 minutes)

Work the remaining words in the trainer's flashcard mode, group reading the pronunciation aloud each time: Elōhīm → Yehōvah → Adōnai → Noach → Shem → Yisra'el → Yerushalayim → Tziyon. Two minutes a word: what English prints, what is hidden, one sentence on why it matters (each word page has the teaching note).

The check (10 minutes)

Run the trainer quiz as a group vote — shout the answer before anyone taps. Misses are the teaching moments; note which words the group missed and re-drill just those.

The send-home (5 minutes)

Hand out the flashcards. The assignment is small on purpose: one word a day, on the fridge, said out loud once at a meal. Next meeting, open with the quiz before anything else.
