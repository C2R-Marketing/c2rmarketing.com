Why ten words work in a group

Most Bible-study members have read English translations for years without ever being shown that the translation is making choices for them. Ten words is small enough to finish in two or three sessions and concrete enough that nobody has to take your word for anything — every claim can be checked against the group's own Bibles in the room.

The format (per session, 30–40 minutes)

Open with one word, not ten. Put the Hebrew word on the screen or the whiteboard. Ask what the group thinks it means before you tell them what English prints. The gap between the guess and the printed word is the lesson — let them sit in it for a moment.

Then show the pair. English rendering on one side, the hidden meaning on the other: Yehōvah behind "LORD", Ro'i inside "my shepherd", Yah inside a song they have sung for years. Groups remember pairs, not lectures.

Close with the trainer. Run the quiz as a group — read each word aloud together (the pronunciation is on every card), and let the score be the group's, not an individual's. Hand out the printable flashcards from the pack below for the week between sessions.

Session plan

Session 1 — the names of God: Elōhīm, Yehōvah, Adōnai, Ro'i. Session 2 — the names that are stories: Noach, Shem, Yisra'el. Session 3 — the places and the song: Yerushalayim, Tziyon, Yah — finish by singing the word you finish on.

Ground rules for the leader

Don't oversell: these ten words open a door, they are not a Hebrew course. Say what the words mean, show the group where to verify it, and let curiosity do the rest.
